### Betty & Barney Hill — The Canonical Case (1961)

The first widely documented abduction case. A mixed-race couple driving through New Hampshire lost two hours of time and came back with matching, detailed memories they couldn't explain. The case that gave us "Greys," the Zeta Reticuli star system, and the template every abduction story since has followed.

> 💡 **Fun fact:** Betty Hill later drew a star map from memory that she said the alien navigator had shown her. In 1969 — eight years later — amateur astronomer Marjorie Fish constructed a 3D model of nearby star systems and found a near-perfect match for Betty's map: the Zeta Reticuli system, which wasn't properly catalogued until 1969. Fish's finding was published in *Astronomy* magazine in 1974.

### John E. Mack — The Harvard Psychiatrist Who Wouldn't Look Away

Dr. John Mack won the Pulitzer Prize for biography, was a full professor at Harvard Medical School, and interviewed over 800 people who believed they'd been abducted. He concluded — as a clinician — that their experiences were psychologically real and demanded serious scientific attention. Harvard convened a secret committee to strip him of tenure. They failed.

> 💡 **Fun fact:** The secret Harvard committee investigating Mack cost the university hundreds of thousands of dollars in legal fees, ran for 14 months, and ultimately cleared him with a note that he'd been "a little too enthusiastic." Harvard never published the committee's report. Mack learned the full details from his own emails and memos exchanged with his lawyers — records that are now at the John Mack Institute in Cambridge, MA.
